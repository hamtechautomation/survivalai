What this section is

Britain packs mountains, moors, chalk downs, fens, estuaries and cities into a small island. Weather, water, food and danger change within a few miles. Each regional guide combines an interactive map with the written detail you need to plan:

  • Terrain zones: elevation, ground, shelter, water and hazards for each landscape.
  • Weather patterns: average rainfall and snow-day layers, Met Office station climate data, live rain radar and a live forecast for any point you click.
  • Wildlife, fishing and foraging maps with a season slider.
  • Water sources, hazards, flood-prone areas, mountain rescue teams, lifeboats and A&E, with distance and bearing from any point.
  • Live flood warnings and river levels from the Environment Agency, with a banner whenever a warning is active anywhere in the region.
  • Sea state and estimated tide times on the coast, plus local radio frequencies, utility numbers and a printable location card.

The live layers need a signal. The written guide below each map works offline, so print it or save the page before you need it.

How to Use a Regional Map

  1. Pick a base layer. Terrain (contours), Street, Satellite, or toggle Hillshade to read the shape of the land.
  2. Switch the zone colouring. Terrain zones, Rainfall (average mm a year) or Snow days shows where weather hits hardest.
  3. Turn on Live rain radar to see rain moving in. It shows the last two hours and loops.
  4. Toggle the category chips: wildlife and game, fishing, foraging, water, hazards, flood areas, rescue, summits and towns.
  5. Drag the Season slider. Anything out of season (closed fishing, fruit not yet ripe, migrant birds gone) fades out.
  6. Click or tap anywhere to get:
    • grid reference (with a copy button), coordinates and elevation;
    • the terrain-zone profile;
    • the live forecast with cold, heat, ice, storm and wind advice;
    • flood warnings within 10 km and the nearest river gauge;
    • waves and tide estimates if you're near the coast;
    • the nearest rescue team, A&E and water source.
  7. 🖨️ Print card prints that location's profile for your grab bag.

Planning aid only

Zone boundaries are deliberately simplified, and points are approximate. Always navigate with a current OS map and compass. In an emergency call 999 (or 112).

The Same Rules Everywhere

  • Access: in England and Wales you may walk on mapped open access land and public rights of way. Wild camping needs the landowner's permission everywhere except parts of Dartmoor (Scotland and Northern Ireland differ; see each region).
  • Hunting, trapping and fishing law is national: see Hunting & Trapping and Fishing.
  • Water: treat all of it. See Water.
  • Rescue: for mountain, moor or cave rescue call 999 → Police → ask for Mountain Rescue (or Cave Rescue). At sea or on the coast call 999 → Coastguard.
  • Signals & radio: licence-free radios, frequencies and rescue signals are the same everywhere in the UK: see Signals & Radio.
