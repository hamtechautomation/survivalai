The essentials

Two landscapes. The Dark Peak (north) is high, featureless gritstone and peat moor: Kinder Scout 636 m, Bleaklow 633 m. The White Peak (south) is a limestone plateau of walled fields and deep, wooded dales. Fog and peat get people lost. Dozens of aircraft wrecks on Bleaklow and Kinder show how quickly the cloud comes down. Navigate by map, compass and timing. Wildfire country. Dry springs and summers bring moorland fires (Saddleworth Moor 2018 burned for three weeks). Never light a fire or barbecue on the moors. Caves and mines: the White Peak is riddled with shafts and flooding caves. Call 999 → Police → Mountain Rescue (or Cave Rescue). Cities on the doorstep: Manchester to the west, Sheffield to the east. In a crisis, roads like the A57 and A628 jam, and the moors fill with people.

1. The Lie of the Land

  • The Dark Peak:
    • Kinder Scout and Bleaklow: flat-topped peat plateaux cut by groughs (peat trenches up to 3 m deep), ringed by gritstone edges.
    • North of them: Black Hill and the Saddleworth/Holme Moss moors.
  • The Eastern Moors & Edges: gritstone cliffs from Stanage to Birchen Edge above the Derwent valley, with heather moor behind.
  • The White Peak: a limestone plateau of walled fields around Buxton, Bakewell and Tideswell. Dovedale, Lathkill, Monsal and the Wye cut deep wooded dales into it.
  • South West Peak: the Roaches, Shutlingsloe and Axe Edge. Five rivers rise on Axe Edge.
  • Upper Derwent: Howden, Derwent and Ladybower reservoirs in a forested valley.
  • City fringes:
    • West: the mill valleys of Glossop, New Mills and Whaley Bridge.
    • East: Sheffield's western suburbs and Chesterfield.

2. Weather Patterns

  • The Dark Peak is the first high ground the Atlantic weather meets after the Cheshire plain: cloud, rain and gales. Buxton (299 m) averages 1,344 mm of rain a year on 172 days. Sheffield, in the eastern rain shadow, averages 832 mm.
  • Snow:
    • The A57 Snake Pass, A628 Woodhead and A537 Cat and Fiddle regularly close.
    • Buxton is often Derbyshire's first town to get snow.
  • Temperature inversions fill the dales and the Hope Valley with fog and frost on still winter nights, while the tops sit in sunshine.
  • Drought and fire: peat dries out in rainless springs. Fires can smoulder underground for weeks.
  • Flash floods: heavy rain on steep, peaty catchments raises streams in minutes. In August 2019 the spillway of Toddbrook Reservoir failed after days of heavy rain, and 1,500 people were evacuated from Whaley Bridge.

3. Zones, Food, Water & Shelter

ZoneSurvival character
Dark Peak & northern moorsPeat water everywhere (filter and treat), almost no shelter or food, serious navigation. Red grouse; mountain hares (England's only population; protected).
Eastern Moors & EdgesRed deer roam freely (keep well away in the Oct–Nov rut); boulders for wind shelter; few streams on top.
White PeakRabbits on limestone grassland; trout and grayling in the Wye, Lathkill and Dove (mostly private fly fishing; the Wye has rare wild-breeding rainbow trout); wild garlic and hazelnuts in the dales. Streams vanish underground in dry weather.
South West PeakWet moor and sheltered valleys; trout in the Dane.
Upper DerwentForest for shelter and fuel (with permission); reservoir water (treat it; never swim: cold water and draw-off towers kill).
City fringesMost people and resources, and the most competition. Canals and rivers are polluted.

Free water: St Ann's Well in Buxton is a public tap of natural mineral spring water, fed by the same thermal spring water that Buxton is famous for.

6. Rescue & Local Information

Rescue:

  • 999 → Police → Mountain Rescue / Cave Rescue. The Peak District Mountain Rescue Organisation coordinates the volunteer teams: Edale, Kinder, Glossop, Buxton, Derby, Oldham and Woodhead. The Derbyshire Cave Rescue Organisation handles caves and mines.
  • Give a grid reference and stay put. See Signals & Radio.

Local information:

  • A&E:
    • Northern General, Sheffield (major trauma centre);
    • Stepping Hill, Stockport;
    • Chesterfield Royal;
    • Macclesfield;
    • Tameside General.
  • Local radio:
    • BBC Radio Derby: 104.5, 95.3 (Bakewell and Matlock), 96.0 (Buxton).
    • BBC Radio Sheffield: 88.6, 104.1, 94.7.
    • BBC Radio Manchester: 95.1 (including Glossop and New Mills), 104.6 (Saddleworth).
  • Utilities:
    • Power: three network operators meet here, and 105 connects you to the right one.
    • Water: Severn Trent, Yorkshire Water or United Utilities, depending on where you are.

7. Bugging Out in the Peak

Expect:

  • Gridlock on the A6, A57, A628 and A623, plus the Hope Valley road and rail line.
  • Crowds: in a city emergency, a lot of people will have the same idea of "heading for the hills".

Better routes on foot:

  • the Pennine Way;
  • trails along old railway lines: the Monsal, Tissington and High Peak trails, and the Longdendale Trail (which follows the old Woodhead railway line);
  • canal towpaths.

Where to base yourself:

  • Best long-term ground: the sheltered White Peak dales and the Derwent valley, which have springs, woodland, farmland and limestone soils.
  • Too exposed to live on: the Dark Peak plateaux.

8. Kit Adjustments for the Peak

  • Navigation: OS Explorer OL1 (Dark Peak) and OL24 (White Peak), a compass, and practice at timing and pacing.
  • Gaiters or high boots for bog. Spare socks.
  • Head torch and a spare: the moors are pitch dark, and walks overrun in winter.
  • Water filter: peat water clogs cheap filters, so pre-filter through a cloth.

9. Collapse Collapse Protocol: Peak District

🔴
COLLAPSE PROTOCOL: Disclaimer

This describes options only in a long-term breakdown of law and supply. Today, trespass, poaching and taking water or game without permission are offences. Read the site-wide disclaimer.

  • The reservoirs (Derwent, Ladybower, Longdendale, Woodhead) supply millions of people downstream. In a collapse they become strategic sites, and probably guarded ones.
  • The White Peak has the soils, springs and sheltered dales for small-scale farming. The Dark Peak can only support sheep and grouse.
  • Fuel: Upper Derwent forests and dale woodland. Coal was once mined on the western fringe.
  • Watch the city edges. Both Manchester and Sheffield are within a day's walk of the moors.
