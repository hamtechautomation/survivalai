What to Buy

The best emergency kit is the kit you already use: a head torch you wear to fix the car, a camping stove you cook on in summer, a flask you take to work. It gets used, so it works, and you know how to use it. Add a short list of things you buy once and keep for emergencies, and skip the gimmicks.

1. Four Rules for Buying

  1. Buy what you’ll use. Everyday kit gets charged, mended and understood. Kit in a sealed box gets flat batteries and forgotten instructions.
  2. Spend on what keeps you alive. Alarms, water, warmth and light. Buy these properly, from shops you trust, marked to the right safety standard.
  3. Cheap is fine for the rest. Supermarket tins, plain bleach, a basic bucket. Branding adds nothing.
  4. Buy slowly. A little each week, with a list (§5), beats a panic shop. And never strip shelves in a crisis: the stores you build calmly are why you don’t need to.

4. Buying Electricals and Safety Kit Safely

  • Buy from retailers you trust. Fake chargers, power banks and alarms sold on marketplaces are a real fire risk. Look for the UKCA or CE mark and a UK address for the seller.
  • Check the standard on the box: alarms (BS EN 14604, BS EN 50291), fire blankets (BS EN 1869), extinguishers (BS EN 3).
  • Fire extinguishers are optional. Your first job in a fire is to get everyone out. If you buy one, learn how to use it; powder extinguishers make a choking cloud indoors.
  • Battery capacity: compare watt-hours (Wh), not just mAh. Power stations quote usable Wh; their output in winter cold is lower.
  • Water filters: trust only claims backed by independent testing. Filters remove bacteria and parasites; most don’t remove viruses or chemicals.
  • Generators: only if you have somewhere outdoors to run one, never in a flat. See generator safety.

7. Keep It Working

  • Test every smoke and CO alarm
  • Charge the power bank, lantern, head torches and power station
  • Swap the stored tap water
  • Eat and replace food near its date
  • Check medicines, first aid kit and glasses
  • Run the stove for a minute; check the gas
  • Check the radio works
  • Update the documents and the household plan
