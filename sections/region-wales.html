The essentials

Wet, steep and close to the sea. Capel Curig in Eryri averages about 2,700 mm of rain a year. Nowhere in Wales is far from the sea, and the weather changes fast. Welsh law is stricter than English law in places.

  • Snares and glue traps are banned outright (since 17 October 2023).
  • Every salmon you catch must go back (since 2020).
  • Wild camping always needs the landowner's permission.

Coal tips: there are more than 2,000 disused tips in Wales, mostly above the Valleys. After prolonged heavy rain, watch for cracks, springs and movement. Rescue: call 999 → Police → Mountain Rescue. On the coast, ask for the Coastguard. Helicopters fly from Caernarfon and St Athan. Flood warnings come from Natural Resources Wales (NRW), not the Environment Agency: Floodline 0345 988 1188.

1. The Lie of the Land

  • Eryri (Snowdonia): Yr Wyddfa (Snowdon, 1,085 m), the Glyderau, Tryfan and the Carneddau in the north. The rougher, quieter Moelwynion, Rhinogydd, Cadair Idris and Aran are in the south.
  • The Cambrian Mountains: the empty moorland heart of Wales around Pumlumon, where the Severn and Wye rise, and the Elan Valley reservoirs.
  • Bannau Brycheiniog (the Brecon Beacons): grassy sandstone escarpments, including Pen y Fan (886 m), the highest point in southern Britain. The Black Mountains are to the east, and Waterfall Country is to the south.
  • The South Wales Valleys: steep former mining valleys with towns on the valley floors and spoil tips on the slopes.
  • The coasts: Ynys Môn (Anglesey), the Llŷn Peninsula, Cardigan Bay, the Pembrokeshire cliffs and islands, Gower, and the Severn Estuary.
  • The Marches: the border hills and the Wye and upper Severn valleys along Offa's Dyke.

2. Weather Patterns

  • The mountains make their own weather. Atlantic air rises over Eryri and the Beacons and drops its rain: Capel Curig gets roughly three times Anglesey's rainfall, though they're only 40 km apart.
  • Extremes: the Wales record high is 37.1 °C at Hawarden (18 July 2022) and the record low is −23.3 °C at Rhayader (21 January 1940).
  • Heat kills here too. In July 2013, three Army reservists died of heat illness on a march in the Beacons. Carry plenty of water on hot days, start early, and turn back if anyone stops sweating or becomes confused (see Fire & Warmth: heatwave).
  • Winter on the tops usually runs from about November to March in Eryri: ice on the paths, cornices, and hill fog.

4. What's Different in Wales

Most of the law is shared with England, including firearms and shotgun certificates, game seasons, the Sunday and Christmas Day game ban, and the rod licence. These rules differ:

TopicThe rule in Wales
Snares & glue trapsBanned since 17 October 2023 (Agriculture (Wales) Act 2023). Wales was the first UK nation with a total ban.
Salmon & sea troutAll salmon caught by rod must be released (since January 2020). Sea trout over 60 cm, and on most rivers all sea trout caught before 1 May, must also go back. Selling rod-caught salmon is an offence. You still need a rod licence (13+) and permission.
Wild campingLandowner permission is always needed. Unlike England (Dartmoor) and Scotland, there is no exception. In Eryri, camping in car parks or on verges is never allowed.
Who regulatesNatural Resources Wales (NRW) does in Wales what the Environment Agency, Natural England and the Forestry Commission do in England: floods, fisheries byelaws, wildlife licences and public forests.
LanguageSigns are bilingual, and many OS map names are Welsh only (see §9).

7. Floods & Storms

  • Storm Dennis (15–16 February 2020):
    • 1,498 properties flooded in Rhondda Cynon Taf alone.
    • At Pontypridd the River Taf reached 5.32 m, its highest since the gauge opened in 1970, and at least 158 properties flooded in the town.
    • Landslips came down across the Valleys, including at the Tylorstown coal tip.
  • Narrow valleys flood fast. In the Valleys and Eryri, rivers can rise several metres within hours of heavy rain. Never drive or walk through floodwater.
  • Warnings: NRW updates flood alerts and warnings every 15 minutes at naturalresources.wales/flooding, with river levels and a 5-day flood outlook. Sign up free, or call Floodline 0345 988 1188.

8. Rescue & Local Information

  • 999 → Police → Mountain Rescue. Wales has volunteer teams across the country:
    • North: Llanberis (one of the busiest in England and Wales), Ogwen Valley, Aberglaslyn, South Snowdonia, Aberdyfi, NEWSAR, and North Wales Cave Rescue.
    • South: Brecon, Central Beacons, Longtown, Western Beacons, and South & Mid Wales Cave Rescue.
  • HM Coastguard search and rescue helicopters fly from Caernarfon and St Athan for both mountain and sea rescues.
  • Major trauma: the University Hospital of Wales (Cardiff) is the major trauma centre for South Wales. Emergency departments are on the map.

10. Bugging Out in Wales

Pinch points:

  • the A55 along the north coast and the M4 in the south, the only fast roads;
  • the A470 north–south through the middle;
  • the Menai bridges to Anglesey;
  • the Severn bridges to England.

Snow and floods close mountain passes (the A5 at Ogwen, the A4086 Llanberis Pass, the A470 at Storey Arms).

Where to go:

  • The south-east cities hold most of the population. In a crisis, expect traffic heading west and north towards "the countryside".
  • Long-term base: sheltered, low ground with woodland, streams and access to the sea. Pembrokeshire, Ceredigion and the Llŷn have the best shore food. The Marches have the best farmland and deer. The Valleys' woods are close to fuel but also to people.
  • Avoid basing yourself on the high Carneddau, Rhinogydd, Cambrian moor or the Beacons escarpment, below a coal tip, or on a valley floor that floods.

11. Kit Adjustments for Wales

  • Waterproofs that really work: a waterproof jacket and overtrousers, plus spare insulation in a dry bag. Wet and windy, rather than very cold, is the main killer here.
  • Winter on Yr Wyddfa and the Glyderau: an ice axe, crampons, and the skills to use them.
  • Maps: OS Explorer OL17 (Snowdon), OL18 (Harlech, Porthmadog & Bala), OL23 (Cadair Idris), OL12 and OL13 (Brecon Beacons west and east), 164 (Gower) and OL35/OL36 (Pembrokeshire), plus a compass.
  • A tide table for any coastal walk: Worm's Head, the estuaries and the islands.
  • Water filter and treatment: sheep and old mines mean all water needs treating.
  • Home: a sandbag or flood-barrier plan if you're on a valley floor; sign up for NRW warnings; solar and batteries for long power cuts in rural areas.

12. Collapse Collapse Protocol: Wales

🔴
COLLAPSE PROTOCOL: Disclaimer

This describes options only in a long-term breakdown of law and supply. Today, setting snares, keeping salmon, poaching and camping without permission are offences or trespass in Wales. Read the site-wide disclaimer.

  • Water is plentiful; food and dry warmth are the problem. Hill land is mostly sheep pasture. Arable land is limited to the Marches, Anglesey, Pembrokeshire and the coastal lowlands.
  • Protein: sheep are everywhere, though they're someone's livestock. The coasts give fish, crab and shellfish all year (with no biotoxin testing, mussels become a gamble). The Marches have deer. Rivers hold trout and sewin.
  • Fuel: vast conifer plantations. Coal seams outcrop in the Valleys, but digging into old workings risks gas, collapse and flooding. Don't.
  • Shelter: stone farmhouses, chapels and slate-roofed outbuildings are built for this climate. Keep away from valley floors that flood and from slopes below coal tips.
  • Community: hill farms, fishing villages and Welsh-speaking communities have deep local knowledge and long memories. Speaking even a little Welsh, and bringing a skill, will help.
