First 24 Hours

When something goes wrong, the first day decides most of what follows. These players walk through six common emergencies hour by hour, from the first minute to the all-clear. They are short on purpose: practise them now, and on the day you will already know the order.

1. Power cut

The most common emergency in the UK. Most power cuts are over in a few hours, but storms can leave homes without power for days. Remember that gas central heating stops too.

⛔
Do not
  • Don’t use candles near curtains or leave them lit.
  • Don’t run a generator, barbecue or patio heater indoors or in a garage: carbon monoxide kills.
  • Don’t open the freezer to check it.
👁️
Watch for

Cold in older people, babies and anyone ill: shivering, confusion, drowsiness. Get them warm and get help.

More: Keeping warm · Fridge and freezer · Light and generator safety · Grid-down playbook

2. Flood warning

The Environment Agency (and its Scottish, Welsh and Northern Irish equivalents) issues three levels of warning. The time to act is the Flood Warning, not when water reaches the door.

⛔
Do not
  • Don’t walk, cycle or drive through flood water.
  • Don’t touch electrics or switches with wet hands or while standing in water.
  • Don’t let children play in flood water.
👁️
Watch for

Stomach upsets after contact with flood water, and cuts that get red, hot or swollen.

More: Flood playbook · Hardening the house · Grab bag

3. Gas leak or CO alarm

A gas leak or a carbon monoxide alarm needs the same first minute: fresh air, gas off, everyone out, then the free 24-hour emergency line from outside.

⛔
Do not
  • Don’t ignore a CO alarm: carbon monoxide has no smell.
  • Don’t go back in to fetch things.
👁️
Watch for

Carbon monoxide poisoning: headache, dizziness, nausea, tiredness, confusion. Symptoms that ease when you leave the house are a warning sign.

More: Finding the gas valve · Gas in vans · CO alarms

4. Water off or unsafe

Burst mains, frozen pipes and contamination can cut off water for days. The order matters: drinking water first, then hygiene, then flushing.

⛔
Do not
  • Don’t drink from radiators or the heating system.
  • Don’t use a hosepipe to share water from a neighbour’s outside tap without asking.
👁️
Watch for

Diarrhoea and vomiting if anyone drank unsafe water: small sips of rehydration solution often.

More: Water hidden in your house · Bucket toilet · Treating water

5. Told to evacuate

When the police or fire service tell you to leave, leave. Evacuations are ordered because staying is more dangerous than going.

⛔
Do not
  • Don’t wait to see what happens.
  • Don’t go back for belongings.
👁️
Watch for

People who can’t leave on their own: older neighbours, disabled people. Tell the police or fire service if someone is still inside.

More: Grab bag · Household plan · Staying or leaving

6. Stuck in snow

Every winter, drivers are stranded overnight on UK roads. Staying with the car, keeping its exhaust clear and staying warm keeps you alive until help arrives.

⛔
Do not
  • Don’t sit with the engine running and the exhaust buried.
  • Don’t walk off into a blizzard.
👁️
Watch for

Hypothermia: shivering, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness. Share body heat and get help.

More: Winter car kit · Winter in the van · Hypothermia
