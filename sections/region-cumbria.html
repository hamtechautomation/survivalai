The essentials

Wettest place in England. Seathwaite in Borrowdale gets about 3,000 mm of rain a year, and the UK 24-hour rainfall record fell at Honister Pass: 341.4 mm on 5 December 2015 (Storm Desmond). Becks become torrents within an hour. The fells are proper mountains. Scafell Pike (978 m) and Helvellyn (950 m) are winter mountaineering terrain from November to March: ice axe and crampons, not trainers. Morecambe Bay sands kill. Tides race in over the flats and the channels shift. Cross only with the official King's Guide. Rescue: call 999 → Police → Mountain Rescue. Cumbria's volunteer teams are among the busiest in Britain. For the lakes, coast and the bay, ask for the Coastguard. Wild camping needs permission (it's often tolerated high on the fells, discreetly and for one night). No fires.

1. The Lie of the Land

  • The Lake District fells:
    • a radiating dome of volcanic and slate mountains (Scafell, Helvellyn, Skiddaw ranges);
    • sixteen major lakes in glaciated valleys (Windermere, Ullswater, Derwentwater, Wastwater);
    • narrow ridges, crags and scree.
  • South Lakes: lower, wooded fells around Windermere, Coniston and Grizedale.
  • West Cumbrian coast: a narrow industrial coastal plain (Workington, Whitehaven) and the Sellafield nuclear site.
  • Solway Plain: flat farmland, peat mosses and saltmarsh around Carlisle and the Solway Firth.
  • Eden Valley: a broad red-sandstone valley between the Lakes and the Pennines, in the Lakes' rain shadow.
  • North Pennines: high, bleak moorland. Cross Fell (893 m) is the highest point in the Pennines.
  • Howgills & Shap Fells: smooth, steep grassy fells crossed by the M6.
  • Morecambe Bay & Furness: vast tidal sands and estuaries, plus the Barrow and Cartmel peninsulas.
  • Bewcastle Fells: remote border moorland at the edge of Kielder Forest.

2. Weather Patterns

PatternWhat it means
Atlantic fronts & "atmospheric rivers"Moist south-westerlies hit the fells first and rain out. Storm Desmond (Dec 2015) flooded 5,200 homes across Cumbria and Lancashire. The previous 24-hour record was also Cumbrian: 316.4 mm at Seathwaite in Nov 2009.
Rain shadowThe Eden Valley and Carlisle get a third to half of central Lakeland's rain: the best growing land and drying weather.
Mountain weatherSummits are often in cloud, 8–10 °C colder than the valleys, and twice as windy. Great Dun Fell (847 m) averages −2.4 °C on January nights and just 12.5 °C on July days.
The Helm WindEngland's only named wind: a violent north-easterly that roars down the Cross Fell escarpment into the Eden Valley, often under a stationary "helm" cloud. It can blow for days.
SnowFell tops can hold snow and cornices from November to April. The A591 Dunmail Raise, Kirkstone, Honister, Hardknott and Wrynose passes and the M6 at Shap are snow-prone.

Use the fell-top forecasts

The Met Office Mountain Weather Information Service and, in winter, the Lake District Weatherline (with a fell-top assessor who climbs Helvellyn daily) tell you what the summits are really like.

4. Zones, Food, Water & Shelter

ZoneSurvival character
High fellsWater everywhere (treat it: sheep and deer). Almost no food above the intake walls apart from bilberries (Jul–Aug). Stone sheepfolds for wind shelter. Brutal winters.
South LakesThe kindest ground: woods for shelter and fuel, lakes for fish (pike, perch), roe and red deer in Grizedale, wild garlic and hazelnuts.
West coastShore fishing (bass, cod in winter), cliff-top rabbits. Sellafield is a no-go zone in any incident.
Solway PlainFarmland and wildfowl (much of it protected reserves). Exposed. Flood-prone Carlisle.
Eden ValleyThe best long-term base: drier, fertile, with salmon and trout rivers, woodland and farms. Watch the Helm Wind.
North PenninesArctic-like tops, old lead mines (contaminated water near spoil), almost no shelter.
Morecambe BayFish and shellfish, but the sands are deadly. Cockling needs a permit, and shellfish beds can be toxic.

Wildlife:

  • Protected (watch only): red squirrels (Whinlatter, Grasmere and Bewcastle woods), ospreys at Bassenthwaite in summer, barnacle geese on the Solway.
  • Deer: red deer at Martindale (England's oldest native herd), Haweswater and Thirlmere; roe deer everywhere wooded.

Fishing:

  • River Eden: one of England's best salmon rivers.
  • Lune: salmon and sea trout.
  • Windermere: pike, perch and the Ice Age relic Arctic char, which has special rules.
  • Protected fish: vendace and schelly live only in a few Cumbrian lakes and must never be taken.

5. Hazards

HazardWhereWhat to do
Narrow ridgesStriding Edge (Helvellyn), Sharp Edge (Blencathra)Fatal falls most years, especially on wet or icy rock. Take the walkers' paths instead in poor conditions.
Winter mountainsAll high fells, Nov–AprIce axe, crampons and the skills to use them; cornices on east-facing edges.
Hill fogEverywhereNavigation errors lead walkers off crags (e.g. Scafell's Broad Stand area). Use map, compass and timing.
Spate riversAll becks after rainNever wade a beck in spate: wait, or go round by a bridge.
Cold waterAll lakesWater stays below 15 °C for much of the year. Cold-water shock kills strong swimmers.
Morecambe Bay & Solway sandsSouth and north coastsTides faster than you can run; quicksand. In 2004, 23 cockle pickers drowned in the bay. Cross only with the King's Guide.
MinesConiston Coppermines, Greenside, NentheadOpen shafts and unstable levels: keep out.
Mountain passesHardknott & Wrynose (1-in-3), Kirkstone, HonisterImpassable in snow. Don't trust a satnav.
SellafieldWest coastIn a nuclear incident, follow Emergency Alerts and official instructions (shelter indoors).

6. Floods

  • Carlisle: January 2005 and December 2015.
  • Cockermouth and Keswick: November 2009 and December 2015.
  • Kendal, Appleby and Glenridding: 2015.

Sign up for Environment Agency flood warnings, keep a grab bag upstairs, and never drive through floodwater. The map shows live warnings near any point you tap.

7. Rescue & Local Information

Mountain rescue:

  • 999 → Police → Mountain Rescue. Cumbria's 12 volunteer mountain rescue teams are coordinated by the Lake District Search & Mountain Rescue Association. Bay Search & Rescue covers Morecambe Bay.
  • Give a grid reference, then stay put. Your phone sends its location automatically when you call 999. See Signals & Radio for the six-blast whistle signal.

Coast and lakes:

  • Coastguard (999) covers lakes, the coast and the bay.
  • RNLI lifeboats: Silloth, Workington, St Bees, Barrow and Windermere (an inland lake station).

Local information:

  • A&E: Cumberland Infirmary (Carlisle), West Cumberland Hospital (Whitehaven), Furness General (Barrow). Westmorland General in Kendal is an urgent treatment centre, not a full A&E.
  • Local radio, BBC Radio Cumbria: 95.6 (north), 96.1 (south), 95.2 (Kendal), 104.1 (Whitehaven), 104.2 (Windermere).
  • Utilities:
    • power cut: 105 (Electricity North West);
    • water: United Utilities;
    • Local Resilience Forum: Cumbria LRF.

8. Bugging Out in Cumbria

Pinch points:

  • the M6 over Shap;
  • the A66 over Stainmore;
  • the A591 through the central Lakes;
  • the A595 coast road;
  • Carlisle's bridges.

Where to go:

  • Away from the lakes and roads, people thin out fast. The Eden Valley, Bewcastle and the Howgills are the quietest.
  • Long-term base: the Eden Valley has fertile land, salmon rivers, woods and the drier climate. The South Lakes woods have shelter, fuel and fish.
  • Avoid the fells and low-lying areas as a base: the high fells for winter living; the Sellafield area; low Carlisle and Cockermouth in winter floods.

9. Kit Adjustments for Cumbria

  • Genuinely waterproof everything: a jacket and trousers with taped seams, dry bags inside the pack, and two spare warm layers.
  • Winter on the fells: an ice axe, crampons, goggles, a bivvy bag and a flask, plus winter skills training.
  • Maps: OS Explorer OL4–OL7 (Lake District), OL19 (Howgills) and OL31 (North Pennines). Use a waterproof map case, because paper maps turn to mush here.
  • Midge net and repellent from June to September.
  • Coast: tide tables, and never venture onto the bay sands alone.

10. Collapse Collapse Protocol: Cumbria

🔴
COLLAPSE PROTOCOL: Disclaimer

This describes options only in a long-term breakdown of law and supply. Today, trespass, poaching and taking water or game without permission are offences. Read the site-wide disclaimer.

  • Water is never the problem here; food and warmth are. Herdwick sheep are hefted to their fells and hardy enough to live on the open fell year-round with little extra feed. In a collapse, flocks and the skills of hill farmers are the county's real wealth.
  • Fuel: Grizedale, Whinlatter, Ennerdale and Kielder forests. Peat burns poorly and slowly.
  • Fish: the Eden and Lune runs, lake pike and perch, and the coast. Nets and fish traps work in lakes and estuaries (see Fishing).
  • Avoid:
    • the high fells in winter;
    • the bay sands without local knowledge;
    • any area downwind of Sellafield in a radiological incident;
    • flood-prone valley towns.
  • Community: valley farming communities (Borrowdale, Eskdale, Wasdale, the Eden villages) have centuries of self-sufficiency. Bring skills to trade.
